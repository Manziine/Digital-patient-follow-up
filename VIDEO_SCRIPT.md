# DPFCSS Video Presentation Script
**Goal:** 5-10 minutes. Speak clearly, at a moderate pace. 
**Before Recording:** Open your live website (https://dpfcss-frontend-y0in.onrender.com/) and have the login page ready on your screen.

---

## 1. Introduction (0:00 - 1:00)
**[Action: Show the Landing Page or Login Screen in your browser]**

"Hello, my name is Arnold Manzi. Welcome to my final Software Prototype demonstration. Today, I am presenting the **Digital Patient Follow-Up and Care Support System**, or DPFCSS.

This system is a full-stack healthcare web application. Its primary description and purpose is to bridge the communication and care gap between healthcare providers and their patients immediately after a hospital discharge."

## 2. The Problem Statement (1:00 - 2:00)
**[Action: Keep the screen steady, you can scroll slightly if you want to show design]**

"The core **problem statement** my system addresses is the lack of structured, continuous post-consultation follow-up in developing healthcare systems. 

**Why is this a problem?** 
Currently, when patients leave the hospital, they frequently fail to adhere to their medication schedules or miss their follow-up appointments. If they experience new symptoms or side effects, they have no direct, simple way to contact their doctor without traveling all the way back to the clinic. This lack of communication leads to poor health outcomes, high hospital readmission rates, and overwhelmed healthcare facilities."

## 3. The Proposed Solution (2:00 - 3:00)
**[Action: Begin typing `provider@demo.com` into the login box]**

"My **proposed solution** is this centralized digital platform. DPFCSS solves the problem by providing a secure online space where doctors can digitally track patient progress, schedule continuous appointments, and manage prescriptions. 

Most importantly, it provides a built-in messaging system so patients can ask health questions securely from their homes, effectively acting as a digital clinic. It is optimized to be fast and lightweight for environments with low internet bandwidth."

## 4. The Demo: Provider Actor (3:00 - 5:00)
**[Action: Click 'Login' as the Provider]**

"I will now demonstrate the system, ensuring the prototype reflects the functionalities indicated in my Software Requirements Specification (SRS) and system design. 

As defined in my system design, one of the main actors is the **Healthcare Provider**. I am logging in now as a provider using secure JWT authentication. 

**[Action: Hover over or click on the Patients List in the Dashboard]**
Here on the Provider Dashboard, I can see a list of my assigned patients. The design captures the functional requirements by giving me an immediate overview of who needs attention.

**[Action: Click on 'Appointments' or a specific Patient Profile]**
I can actively schedule a new follow-up appointment or review their current medical status. 

**[Action: Click on 'Messages']**
Finally, here is the messaging center. If a patient reaches out with an issue, I can quickly reply to them here, preventing an unnecessary hospital visit and ensuring continuous care."

## 5. The Demo: Patient Actor (5:00 - 6:30)
**[Action: Click 'Log Out', then type `patient@demo.com` and log back in]**

"Now, let's look at the second main actor: **The Patient**.

**[Action: Show the Patient Dashboard]**
When the patient logs in, their dashboard is completely tailored to them. They can easily see their upcoming tasks and active prescriptions. It is designed to be highly intuitive.

**[Action: Click on 'Messages' or 'Appointments' for the Patient]**
They can confirm when their next clinic visit is, and if they feel sick, they simply use the messaging tab to securely chat with their doctor. All of these required functionalities are working seamlessly as outlined in my SRS."
